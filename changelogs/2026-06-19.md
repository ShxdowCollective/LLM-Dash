# Changelog — June 20, 2026

## New Models

### DeepSeek V4-Pro
DeepSeek's flagship open-weight model arrives on the dashboard after its April 24 release, filling a major gap in the catalog. V4-Pro is a 1.6-trillion-parameter Mixture-of-Experts model with 49B parameters activated per token, supporting a 1M-token context window under the MIT license. It scores [80.6% on SWE-bench Verified](https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4-Pro) in Think Max mode — the highest published open-weight score, tying with Gemini 3.1 Pro — and [90.1% on GPQA Diamond](https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4-Pro), within a few points of the Claude and Gemini frontiers. On Terminal-Bench 2.0 it posts [67.9%](https://evals.report/models/deepseek-v4-pro), and it holds a Codeforces rating of 3,206, the highest of any publicly ranked model. The three selectable reasoning modes (Non-think, Think High, Think Max) give teams fine-grained control over speed-depth trade-offs. Standard API pricing is $1.74/$3.48 per million tokens, roughly 7–30x cheaper on output than closed competitors at comparable coding performance. At launch V4-Pro is text-only — no native image or audio input — and self-hosting requires a multi-GPU cluster of 4×H200 or equivalent. The MIT license enables fine-tuning, private inference, and air-gapped deployment with no vendor dependency.

### GLM-5.2
Zhipu AI released GLM-5.2 on June 16 under the MIT license, adding another 1M-context open-weight model to the landscape. The 753B-parameter model scores [91.2% on GPQA Diamond](https://tpsreport.news/news/glm-5-2-1m-context-swe-bench) and [62.1% on SWE-bench Pro](https://tpsreport.news/news/glm-5-2-1m-context-swe-bench), placing it between GPT-5.5 and Claude Opus 4.8 on that benchmark. On Terminal-Bench 2.1 it achieves [81.0%](https://tpsreport.news/news/glm-5-2-1m-context-swe-bench), and on AIME 2026 it scores [99.2%](https://tpsreport.news/news/glm-5-2-1m-context-swe-bench). The IndexShare architecture reuses sparse-attention indexers across layers, cutting per-token FLOPs by 2.9× at full 1M context. Flexible reasoning-effort levels let developers trade latency for performance on coding tasks. API pricing is $1.40/$4.40 per million tokens. The MIT license removes export restrictions common in Chinese AI models, and deployment is supported through SGLang, vLLM, and Transformers.

### Mistral Large 3
Mistral shipped the Mistral 3 family on June 18, headlined by Mistral Large 3 — a 675B-parameter sparse MoE model with 41B active parameters, released under Apache 2.0. This is the largest permissively-licensed model to date. It ranks [first among open-weight models on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/318622/20260618/minimax-m3-takes-open-weight-ai-lead-sparse-attention-architecture-now-verified.htm) with a score of 55 and [#2 among open-source non-reasoning models on LMArena](https://tpsreport.news/news/mistral-3-release-large-675b-ministral-models). Pricing is aggressively low at $0.50/$1.50 per million tokens. The model was trained from scratch on 3,000 NVIDIA H200 GPUs and ships with NVFP4 quantization enabling single-node deployment on 8×A100 or 8×H100 via vLLM. Multimodal input (text and image) is natively supported. A reasoning variant is planned for a future release. Availability spans Mistral AI Studio, Amazon Bedrock, Azure Foundry, Hugging Face, and multiple third-party inference providers.

### Kimi K2.7 Code
Moonshot AI announced Kimi K2.7 Code on June 19 as an open-source, coding-focused agentic model built on the same 1T-parameter MoE architecture as Kimi K2.6. The defining design goal is reasoning efficiency: Moonshot claims [roughly 30% fewer thinking tokens](https://www.toolmintx.in/blog/kimi-k2-7-code-moonshot-ai-latest-models) versus K2.6 while scoring higher on coding benchmarks. On Moonshot's own evaluations it shows clear gains — +21.8% on Kimi Code Bench v2 and +31.5% on MLS Bench Lite — though it still trails GPT-5.5 and Claude Opus 4.8 on most rows. Independent testers have [questioned the generalizability](https://www.toolmintx.in/blog/kimi-k2-7-code-moonshot-ai-latest-models) of these gains: on the public KernelBench-Hard benchmark, one researcher found K2.7 Code was "more honest but not more capable" than K2.6, with a kernel result actually regressing. The model is thinking-only (no non-thinking mode) with fixed temperature at 1.0, open weights under a Modified MIT license, and supports 256K context with native image input via the MoonViT encoder.
